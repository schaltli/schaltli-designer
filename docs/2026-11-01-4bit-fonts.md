# 4-bit fonts

Asked for by the user on 2026-11-01, after CamperUI (github.com/NiklasJester/CamperUI,
v1.9.1) ran on the same Waveshare 4": its text looked smoother. Measured on
the framebuffer: CamperUI's Montserrat edges carry 16 levels (LVGL, 4 bits
per pixel), Schaltli's text carries 2 - every font a board draws is a 1-bit
BDF (`src/project/BdfFont.cpp`, designer `lib/bdffont.ts`). Curves step,
and the 1 px strokes of helvR08/12 look thin at the 4"'s 172 ppi. In
screenshots on a PC, where most people first see Schaltli, it reads as
"retro".

Status: draft, approved by the user on 2026-11-01. Decisions 1-4 taken
that day; pre-study done the same day (flash fits, see below); the families
are open.

## Objective

Text on the colour boards is drawn with anti-aliased glyphs: each pixel of a
glyph carries one of 16 coverage levels and is blended with what lies under
it. The designer draws the same bytes the same way, so preview and glass stay
pixel-identical, as they are today.

Users: everyone with a colour board. Nobody has to do anything - their
projects switch over on their own (decision 2).

## Decisions

1. **Several families, as typographies.** The 4-bit fonts come as named
   typographies, like today's Standard / Humanist / Technic, each from a free
   outline font (OFL or Apache). Helvetica and Lucida exist here only as
   u8g2 bitmaps, so the families change.
2. **Every project switches automatically.** The colour boards' typographies
   are replaced, not joined, by 4-bit ones. A project opened in the designer
   moves each text to the 4-bit font that replaces its old one; texts get a
   little wider or narrower and may move.
3. **Android stays on TTF.** The app keeps drawing Roboto through Android;
   this work does not touch it.
4. **The PaperS3 stays on 1 bit.** Its fast partial refresh (`epd_fast`)
   renders grey poorly; it keeps its BDF fonts and typographies.

9. **The families** (user, 2026-11-01, after the previews on the 4"; Source
   Sans 3 "too bold", IBM Plex Sans out, the rest left to Claude):

   | Typography | Caption / Label / Title | Display |
   |---|---|---|
   | Standard | Roboto | Roboto |
   | Humanist | Inter | Inter |
   | Technic | Roboto Condensed | Seven Segment (DSEG7, now 4 bits) |

   Roboto is the Android app's face, so a Standard screen looks alike on
   every device. Inter has the most open shapes of what is left and is drawn
   for small sizes on screens - the nearest to Lucida's role. Atkinson
   Hyperlegible lacks Czech and Polish letters; Montserrat is not needed.
10. **Sizes by cap height** (Claude, delegated): for the 4-bit typographies
    the designer picks the font whose capitals come closest to
    `0.69 × line` - the ratio today's Helvetica has - not its line height.
    Roboto and Inter keep more room around their letters (capitals 0.60 of
    the line); by line height every text would have shrunk. 1-bit
    typographies keep picking by line height, so the PaperS3 does not change.

11. **Font data lives in the DDF, not in projects** (user, 2026-11-01,
    after T5's first step broke test:quick: 120 e2e failures, «Request body
    exceeded 10MB», boards not back from an install in 180 s). Every project
    carries its device's DDF as `embeddedDdfZipBase64`, fonts and all, and
    carried every font's bytes a second time in `project.fonts`. An open
    project keeps them in memory as before; saved, drafted and zipped it
    leaves out each font whose very bytes the DDF holds at the same path, and
    gets them back from it when opened (`lib/project-font-data.ts`). A
    font of the project's own keeps its data.
12. **No DDF in the recovery copy of a deploy to its own board** (user,
    2026-11-01). A deploy's `_source/project.zip` carried the whole DDF as
    `_source/ddf.zip` - 0.3 MB with 1-bit fonts, 1.0-1.25 MB with 4-bit ones,
    in every deploy and over the broker. When the board's announced
    `ddfHash` is the project's, it is left out; recovered, the designer takes
    the DDF the board announces (`resolveDeviceForProject`, the path every
    project without an embedded DDF already takes). This changes
    docs/nested-provenance.md's "the recovery copy carries its DDF" for that
    case. The HIL's harness builds its fixtures the same way.

13. **Characters of two code pages, and a font cache** (user, 2026-11-02,
    after three typographies used up the 4.3B's PSRAM in T9: 26 fonts, 11 KB
    left). The 4-bit fonts are written with the Windows code pages 1250 and
    1252 - German, French, English, Czech, Polish, € — … - and the chart's
    «→ −», not helvR18's 753 glyphs: 271, all three typographies on the 4.3B
    1.7 MB instead of 3.9. (The user named 1250; it lacks French à è ê ù œ,
    so 1252 is in as well.) The firmware loads fonts ahead only while 1 MB
    of PSRAM stays free, and on a screen change lets go of the least recently
    used ones the new screen does not name while it is below.

Assumed, not asked (correct here if wrong):

5. **Pre-rasterised, never at runtime.** Fonts are rasterised once from TTF by
   a build tool in schaltli-firmware (next to `tools/ttf-to-bdf.py`) and
   shipped in the device's DDF. No TrueType rasteriser on the device or in
   the designer: the browser's would not agree with the device's, and "one
   set of bytes, one answer" (device-contract, *Ship the font, don't name
   it*) is what keeps the HIL pixel diff at 0.
6. **Depth per font, not per device.** A DDF font entry says how many bits a
   pixel has (`bpp: 1 | 4`, absent = 1). A monochrome device - an OLED, a
   1-bit e-paper - keeps shipping 1-bit fonts; nothing is lost for it.
7. **One blend, written down once.** Coverage `a` (0-15) mixes foreground and
   background in RGB565 the way the firmware already blends anti-aliased
   pills (`levelglow::blend565`); the designer reproduces that integer
   arithmetic, including the 565 quantisation, so both round the same way.
8. **First boards:** Waveshare 4", 4.3B, Knob 1.8.

## What changes

### Font file

A BDF with one addition, so the parser, the tooling and a diff in review all
stay readable: the property `BITS_PER_PIXEL 4`, and bitmap rows written one
hex digit per pixel (coverage 0-F) instead of one bit per pixel. Everything
else - `BBX`, `DWIDTH`, ascent, descent, `CAP_HEIGHT` - is as today, so
layout, ink extents and the existing off-by-one reproduced in `drawGlyph()`
keep working unchanged.

### DDF (device-contract §1)

`fonts[]` gains `bpp?: 1 | 4` and `replaces?: string[]` - the ids of the old
fonts this one stands in for. `typography[]` keeps its shape; the colour
boards' sets name the new families.

### Designer

- `lib/bdffont.ts` reads 4-bit glyphs and draws them with the blend of
  decision 7 onto what is already on the canvas.
- On opening a project, every `fontId` (and `valueFontId` etc.) that a font of
  the device `replaces` is rewritten to that font, like the Live Text → Text
  migration. The project is marked changed, so the next deploy carries it.
- After the switch, texts that no longer fit their object are listed, the way
  objects the device cannot draw are listed today.

### Firmware

- `BdfFont` stores 4-bit glyphs (two pixels per byte, in PSRAM) and
  `drawGlyph()` blends onto the canvas it draws into.
- A project still on the device from before the update names old font ids.
  The firmware resolves them through `replaces`, so text keeps drawing until
  the next deploy brings the migrated project.
- The colour boards' DDFs drop their 1-bit BDFs once nothing names them.

### Handbook

`handbuch/designer/icons-schriften.md` (typographies, what they look like,
that projects switched), the device pages for 4", 4.3B and Knob, and wherever
the old family names Helvetica / Lucida are quoted.

## Tech stack

Designer: Next.js / TypeScript, canvas renderers in `components/canvas/renderers`.
Firmware: PlatformIO, pioarduino 53.03.10 (Arduino core 3.1, IDF 5.3.2), LVGL
8.4 only for input; text drawn by `ColorScreenRenderer` into `ClippedCanvas16`.
Font build: Python 3 with Pillow (FreeType), as `tools/ttf-to-bdf.py`.

## Project structure

```
schaltli-firmware/tools/ttf-to-bdf.py        → gains --bpp 4
schaltli-firmware/tools/fonts-src/<family>/  → TTF sources + licence
schaltli-firmware/ddf-source*/fonts/         → generated 4-bit BDFs
schaltli-firmware/src/project/BdfFont.*      → 4-bit storage and blending
schaltli-designer/lib/bdffont.ts             → 4-bit parsing and drawing
schaltli-designer/lib/                       → font migration (new module)
schaltli-designer/docs/device-contract.md    → bpp, replaces, the blend
schaltli-designer/e2e/                       → migration + rendering specs
schaltli-designer/hil/*/fixtures/            → pixel-parity fixtures with 4-bit text
```

## Code style

As the surrounding code: comments say why, with the date and the board a
finding came from; names as in `BdfFont` / `bdffont.ts`. The blend lives in
one function on each side, named the same, with the arithmetic spelled out:

```ts
// Same integer arithmetic as the firmware's levelglow::blend565, so the
// preview rounds exactly as the glass does (docs/2026-11-01-4bit-fonts.md, 7).
export function blendCoverage565(under: Rgb565, ink: Rgb565, coverage: number): Rgb565
```

## Testing strategy

- **Pixel parity is the gate.** Each colour board's HIL fixture gets screens
  with 4-bit text: every style and weight, light and dark theme, text over a
  gradient and over a chart, diacritics, `€ — …`. The diff stays 0 / all
  pixels, as today.
- **e2e:** a project with 1-bit font ids opens migrated (ids replaced, project
  marked changed, overflowing texts listed); the starter and demo projects
  migrate without an overflowing text.
- **Unit:** the 4-bit BDF parser (designer and `pio test -e native`), the blend
  on both sides against the same table of values.
- **Device without redeploy:** firmware with new fonts plus an old project on
  the device draws all text (HIL, by uploading the old project first).
- PaperS3 and Android lanes run unchanged and stay green.

## Boundaries

- **Always:** keep the HIL diff at 0; run `npm run test:quick` for every
  change, `test:all` before a release; ship licence files with every font;
  update the handbook in the same piece of work.
- **Ask first:** choice of families (open question 1); dropping glyphs to fit
  flash; moving the DDF out of the app partition; anything that changes the
  PaperS3 or Android.
- **Never:** rasterise TrueType at runtime on a device or in the designer's
  renderer; ship a font without its licence; leave a device unable to draw
  the text of a project it already holds.

## Success criteria

1. On the 4", 4.3B and Knob, glyph edges show intermediate levels (framebuffer
   check: > 2 distinct colours inside a glyph's box on a flat background).
2. HIL pixel diff 0 on all three colour boards for the 4-bit fixtures.
3. Every existing project opens migrated; starter and demo projects have no
   overflowing text afterwards.
4. A board updated to the new firmware with an old project still on it draws
   every text.
5. Each colour board's firmware still fits its app partition with ≥ 10 %
   headroom.
6. A full screen redraw on the 4.3B takes at most 20 % longer than today.
7. PaperS3 and Android unchanged, their lanes green.
8. Handbook updated, `e2e/handbook-labels.spec.ts` green.

## Pre-study (2026-11-01)

Seven candidates rasterised at 4 bits (Pillow/FreeType), laid out glyph by
glyph at integer advances without kerning as a device would, blended with
`blend565`, and shown on the Waveshare 4" itself (a viewer added to the
screenshot build of CamperUI; the framebuffer read back matched the images in
RGB565 exactly). Alongside: today's Standard and Humanist in 1 bit.

**Flash is not the constraint.** One typography at the 4.3B's sizes (each
style's line and one either side, regular and bold, 753 glyphs - today's
helvR18 set):

| Family | zipped 4-bit BDF (flash) | unpacked 4 bpp (PSRAM) |
|---|---|---|
| Roboto | 450 KB | 1.6 MB |
| Roboto Condensed | 426 KB | 1.5 MB |
| Source Sans 3 | 482 KB | 1.3 MB |
| IBM Plex Sans | 400 KB | 1.5 MB |
| Inter | 679 KB | 1.9 MB |
| Montserrat | 602 KB | 2.0 MB |

Three typographies come to 1.2-2 MB. An app slot is 6.25 MB
(`default_16MB.csv`), the firmware ~2.2 MB today, so they fit with the 10 %
headroom of success criterion 5 - no subset, no own partition. The PSRAM
figure is per typography if every size were loaded; the firmware loads the
fonts a screen uses.

**Glyph coverage.** Missing from the 753: mostly Latin Extended-B (Ɔ, Ƨ, …),
irrelevant here - Roboto 285, IBM Plex 269, Source Sans 3 134, Montserrat 41,
Inter 15. Atkinson Hyperlegible lacks 440, including parts of Latin
Extended-A (Czech, Polish, Slovak); its successor *Atkinson Hyperlegible
Next* would have to be checked instead. The DDF's glyph set should be what a
family actually has, so the designer's "does the device have this character?"
keeps one answer.

**Picking a size by line height misleads.** The designer takes the font whose
ascent + descent comes closest to a style's line (`fontFor`). Families with
generous vertical metrics (Source Sans 3, IBM Plex Sans) then come out with
visibly smaller letters than Roboto at the same line. Measured afterwards
(T1): Roboto and Inter alike have capitals of 0.60 of their line, today's
Helvetica 0.67-0.70 - so even Roboto would shrink every text. Decision 10.
