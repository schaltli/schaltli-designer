# Charts

Asked for by the user on 2026-10-16, after comparing Schaltli with CamperUI
(github.com/NiklasJester/CamperUI, v1.7.1): it shows energy over 1/6/24 h
and temperatures over 1/6/12/24 h as curves; Schaltli shows a value only as
it is now. Issue #73.

Status: every decision taken by the user on 2026-10-16 and 2026-10-17;
the pinch gestures (decision 15) proved on the 4.3B on 2026-10-17;
getting to a history page (decision 12) on the screen graph
(`docs/2026-10-17-screen-graph.md`), still a draft.

## Objective

A chart draws one or more values over a span of time: the battery's SoC
over the day, solar power, inside and outside temperature over the night.
It is placed in the designer like any object, drawn the same in the
preview and on every device, and shows the curve the moment a display
starts - after a restart or a deploy as much as after a night switched off.

Users: whoever builds a panel; first the starter project (#56), whose
energy page gets the energy flow over the day (decision 0).

## What is drawn

Charts answer questions asked in a van (worked out with the user on
2026-10-16; collecting everything and leaving the question open was turned
down as too easy):

| Question | Values | Span | Drawn as |
|---|---|---|---|
| Where did the energy come from and go to, today? | the energy diagram's nodes | 24 h | stacked areas, sources above zero, sinks below (decision 0) |
| Will the battery last? | SoC | 24 h, 7 days | line |
| What came in and went out per day? | the energy diagram's nodes | 7 days | bars per day, Wh in and out - the integral of the areas above |
| Warm enough overnight, frost outside? | inside, outside temperature | 24 h | lines, the minimum marked |
| Did the heater hold? | inside, target temperature, heater on/off | 24 h | line over a band of when it ran |
| What did the water do? | fresh, grey water | 24 h; 7 days per day | steps (a tank changes when water is drawn); per day, what it went down by |
| Is the fridge cold? | fridge temperature | 24 h | line, the maximum marked |

Not charted: relay and dimmer states, fan speed, battery voltage.

Four kinds of chart follow from it, and all four come in the first
version (user, 2026-10-16): stacked power areas, lines, bars per day, and
a state band under a line.

0. **The energy page shows the energy flow over the day** (user,
   2026-10-16, from a stacked power chart like Home Assistant's): the
   nodes of an energy diagram (`docs/2026-10-11-energy-diagram.md`) over
   24 h, each source an area above zero and each sink one below, in the
   diagram's own colour roles (`energySun`, `energyGrid`, `energyEngine`,
   `energyLoad`). Made from an energy diagram, the chart takes over its
   nodes' topics once (decision 8) rather than having them set up a
   second time. A step's mean power is exactly
   its energy divided by its length, so the daily bars come from the same
   means and need no recording of their own.

## Decisions

1. **One recorder in the van, not one per display** (user, 2026-10-16).
   A service on the machine that runs the broker collects the history and
   lays it on the broker; a display only draws what it finds there. So
   every display shows the same curve, a display that just started shows
   it at once, and the knob, with little RAM, keeps nothing. It is the
   model of Home Assistant's recorder: collected centrally, read by the
   frontends. Home Assistant has no format for this over MQTT, so the
   format is our own (decision 7).
2. **In RAM, written to the card at most every 30 minutes** (user,
   2026-10-16). The recorder keeps the history in memory and writes it to
   one file every 30 minutes and when it is stopped; it reads it back when
   it starts. About 50 writes a day, whatever the rate of the values - not
   one per value, as Home Assistant's recorder does, which is known to wear
   out SD cards. A van that loses power abruptly loses at most the last 30
   minutes.
3. **Its own small service, always on** (user, 2026-10-17). Not inside the
   designer, which systemd starts on demand and stops when idle
   (`deploy/pekaway-install.sh`), and not a Node-RED flow, which would tie
   it to Pekaway: a Node.js process of its own, a systemd unit installed
   with the designer, on a Pekaway and without one alike.
4. **It records what the deployed charts ask for** (user, 2026-10-16;
   recording every numeric topic was turned down as too easy). On deploy
   the designer lays retained under `schaltli/history/wants/<instance>`
   what that display's charts need: per series its topic (with `#path`),
   its kind and, for a state, what counts as on (decision 8). The recorder
   records the union of all displays' wants and stops a topic no display
   wants any more. A chart has its history from its first deploy on. The
   recorder keeps raw values only; calibration, colours and annotations
   stay on the display.
5. **Three kinds of value** (user, 2026-10-16):
   - **level** (SoC, temperature, humidity, tank): mean, minimum and
     maximum per step.
   - **power** (W, signed): mean per step; a step's mean power times its
     length is its energy, so energy per day needs nothing of its own.
   - **state** (heater, pump): the periods it was on, not a number.

   The designer infers the kind and lets it be overridden (user,
   2026-10-16): from a Home Assistant `device_class` where there is one
   (`power` → power, `temperature`, `humidity`, `battery` → level, a
   binary sensor or switch → state), from the unit (`W` → power), from the
   topic's examples (`on`/`off` → state), else level.
6. **Thirty days, the same for every chart** (user, 2026-10-16). The
   recorder keeps 30 days at 15-minute steps and the last 48 hours at
   1-minute steps, for the 1-hour and 6-hour views. Per topic that is
   2880 + 2880 points of three numbers; 50 topics stay under 4 MB of RAM.
   A chart scrolls back as far as the recorder keeps, no further.
7. **On the broker, retained, by the day** (user, 2026-10-17). A display that
   scrolls fetches only the days it shows, by subscribing to them:
   - `schaltli/history/<topic>/day/<YYYY-MM-DD>`: one calendar day,
     96 steps of `{mean, min, max}` (a state: its on-periods in minutes
     since midnight), `null` for a step nothing arrived in. A mean is weighted by time: a value
holds until the next one, for 15 minutes at most (tasks/charts-recorder-plan.md,
decision 3). A day has 92 or 100 steps on the days the clock changes. Today's is
     published again at the end of each step; a finished day once, and
     cleared after 30 days.
   - `schaltli/history/<topic>/recent`: the last 2 hours at 1-minute
     steps, for the 1-hour view; the 6-hour view uses 5-minute means of
     the same, kept 48 hours under `…/recent6`.
   - `schaltli/history/<topic>/daily`: the last 30 days' values per day
     (energy in and out, decline, rise, mean, minimum, maximum, run time),
     for a chart in `per day` mode.
   - `schaltli/history/now`: the recorder's time and time zone, so a
     display without a clock knows which day is today.

   A day is a few KB, which the knob's MQTT buffer takes.
8. **The chart object** (user, 2026-10-16: "KEINE HARTCODIERTEN
   Diagramme" - a humidity sensor gets its chart by configuration, as
   anything else does). One object type, set up in the designer:

   *The chart as a whole*
   - **Mode**: `time` (a curve over time) or `per day` (one bar per
     calendar day).
   - **Visible span**: none of its own; a chart shows its screen's time
     range (decision 15). In `per day` mode, the number of days shown.
   - **Scrolling**: on or off; on, it reaches back as far as the recorder
     keeps (decision 6). The designer sets it on for the charts of a
     history page it proposes, off elsewhere (decision 12).
   - **Snap**: none, whole hours or whole days; whole days from a 24-hour
     span up.
   - **Left y-axis**: `auto`, `fixed` (min, max) or `at least` (min, max,
     widened when a value falls outside); `auto` starts at 0 when every
     value is ≥ 0.
   - **Right y-axis**: as the left, used once a series is put on it.
   - **Zones**: ranges of one axis tinted in a colour role (SoC below 20 %,
     below 0 °C, humidity above 70 %).
   - **Legend** (user, 2026-10-17): top right, top left, bottom or none.
     The figures of the annotations stand in it.

   *Each series*
   - **Source: a topic, nothing else** (user, 2026-10-17). A chart made
     from a page, or from an energy diagram, gets its controls' or nodes'
     topics (with `#path`), names, colours and units filled in once;
     after that they are the chart's own, and changing the control does
     not change the chart. Following the controls live (proposed on
     2026-10-16) was turned down: two places to keep in step are the
     price of one model that is simple to understand. The one exception
     is the **balance**: the «rest» node of an energy diagram has no
     topic, it is what the measured flows leave over
     (`lib/energy-diagram.ts`). A series of source `balance` is worked
     out by the display, step by step, as minus the sum of the other
     series in its stack; the means of a step add up exactly, so it needs
     no recording.
   - **Name**: the topic's or node's, unless given.
   - **Kind**: level, power or state, inferred and overridable (decision
     5); for a state, **on means** a condition as in a Live Icon.
   - **Factor**: shown value = raw value × factor, with unit and decimals
     - a sensor's mV as V. One unit per series, the same on the axis and
     in the legend: tanks are shown in %, on the axis and in the legend
     alike, not in litres (user, 2026-10-17).
   - **Drawn as**: line, area, steps, stacked (a stack group), band (a
     state) or bars (`per day` mode). A state defaults to a band.
   - **Axis**: left or right.
   - **Colour**: a colour role of the theme, energy roles included; an
     E-ink display tells series apart by hatching.
   - **Range**: off, or the step's minimum and maximum as a faint band
     around the line.
   - **Daily value** (`per day` mode): energy (Wh), decline, rise, mean,
     minimum, maximum or run time; by default energy for power, mean for a
     level, run time for a state. A tank's use is its decline: a refill
     does not count.

   *Annotations* (user, 2026-10-17): six, chosen per series, each worked
   out from what was measured in the visible window, so it follows the
   scrolling - a text outside the chart could not. Where each is drawn:
   - **current** (level, power): large in the legend, always the value
     now, labelled «jetzt», wherever the window is (user, 2026-10-17); and
     on the curve, at the NowPoint. A window that ends before now - a past
     day - shows the NowPoint as a small arrow at its right edge, «→ 36 %»,
     pointing to where it lies. The last value of a past window is not
     shown as current: under the same name it would pass for the value
     now.
   - **min** and **max** (level, power): a point on the curve, with value
     and time below (min) or above (max).
   - **sum** (power only): in the legend, the integral over the window in
     Wh or kWh - energy, not power (user, 2026-10-17). Its bounds are the
     window's, but only as far as there are measurements: snapped to a
     past day it is that day's yield, on today it runs from midnight to
     the NowPoint, and between two snaps it covers exactly the window, so
     the window's start and end are written beside it («16.10. 14:00 –
     17.10. 14:00»). A signed series - a battery charging and
     discharging - shows two figures, in and out, since their net would
     hide both. The power at this moment is **current**, in W; a series
     can show either or both.
   - **run time** (state only): in the legend, how long the state
     was on in the window.
   - **starts** (state only): in the legend, how many runs began
     in the window, and a small triangle above the band where each began.

   Turned down on 2026-10-17 as not worth their cost: mean, change over
   the window, since the last jump, a figure per day under each day's
   name, marked jumps («aufgefüllt +86 L»).
9. **The device decides how it moves** (user, 2026-10-16): a colour
   display draws the curves in, follows a finger, coasts after a flick and
   settles on whole days (within 6 hours of a midnight). Scrolled as far
   right as it goes, the window shows today whole, midnight to midnight
   (user, 2026-10-17): the NowPoint sits at the clock's time - at noon in
   the middle of the screen - and the rest of the day is still empty.
   Day boundaries stay visible: a stronger line at midnight, the day's
   name at the top, alternate days faintly tinted. Charts are drawn at
   the display's own resolution, not as vectors scaled to it. The PaperS3
   does not follow a finger: it draws again when the finger lets go,
   already snapped, in greys and hatching, and otherwise only when a new
   step arrives.
10. **A feature the device declares** (user, 2026-10-17), as local topics are
    (`docs/2026-10-13-local-topics.md`, decision 6): a device draws charts
    once its DDF says so; Deploy sends a project with a chart only to a
    device that does, and brings an older board up to date first.
11. **The designer's preview reads the same topics** (user, 2026-10-17): Live
    shows the recorder's history; Simulation draws curves made from the
    topics' examples. Without a recorder on the broker the Live chart says
    so instead of drawing nothing.

12. **A page's history lies under it, a swipe up away** (user,
    2026-10-17). The normal controls come first; once a page is built, it
    can get a history: a screen of charts belonging to that page.
    - **It is a screen like any other** (user, 2026-10-17), the one
      below its page in the screen graph
      (`docs/2026-10-17-screen-graph.md`): no screen type of its own, no
      action of its own to open it. What the graph gives - swiping up to
      the screen below, the pull tab on an edge with a neighbour - gives
      the history page too.
    - **The designer proposes it** from the page's controls, and each
      series takes over its control's topic (with `#path`), unit, colour
      and the min/max of a Bar or Gauge, once, when it is made (decision
      8); anything can be changed afterwards. A Bar, Gauge or
      value becomes a level, a Dial or Slider a stepped «target» line, a
      Switch a band, an energy diagram stacked power. What changes too
      quickly for 15-minute steps, such as a water pump, is not proposed.
      Two units on one chart give it two axes.
    - **Not every page has one.** A page whose values tell nothing over
      time (relays, dimmers) gets none.
    - **The chart is an ordinary object, not a screen of its own** (user,
      2026-10-17): placed, sized and combined with other objects like a
      Bar or the energy diagram, on any screen. A history page is a screen
      too, which the designer fills with a proposed layout - charts the
      full width, stacked, the pull tab at the top - and which is edited
      like any other afterwards: a text beside a curve, two charts side by
      side on a wide Android screen. The devices draw a chart at its
      x, y, width and height, as every other object; a fixed history
      layout would have needed a layout of its own per device.
    - **Charts on one screen share their time axis**: swiping one scrolls
      them all - the energy flow above, the SoC below.
    - **Scrolling is a chart's property**: the designer sets it on for
      the charts of a history page it proposes, off elsewhere, where a
      horizontal swipe often changes the page. A chart on a normal page
      shows a fixed window - today - and a tap on it goes where swiping up
      goes, to the screen below.
    - **Getting there**: swiping up, to the screen below (screen graph).
      On the history page a horizontal swipe scrolls through time and a
      swipe down returns; on the knob the ring moves through time, mapped
      to «Move the time range» (decision 15).
    - No long press (turned down on 2026-10-17: the swipe does it, and a
      long press would compete with switching).
13. **Names** (user, 2026-10-17): the pulsing point at the clock's time is
    the **NowPoint**. One value drawn over time - line, area, steps, band
    or bars - is a **series**: «Series» in the designer, «Datenreihe» in
    the handbook (not «Reihe», which is the table object «Row»).
14. **Only what was measured; no forecasts for now** (user, 2026-10-17).
    «The water lasts about 2 more days» or «the battery holds until 9»
    depend on what nobody measures: the weather, and why a level changed -
    a flushed tank reads exactly like water used. Answers in words above
    the chart («since the refill the tank went down by 64 L») were turned
    down too: the annotations already say it where it happened.

15. **The time range belongs to the screen, and gestures and buttons
    change it through actions** (user, 2026-10-17). Nothing about zooming
    or moving is built into the chart; it is mapped, as a screen's swipes
    and hardware buttons are (`handbuch/designer/tasten.md`).
    - **The screen's time range.** A screen has one, and every chart on it
      shows that range, so they are always in step. The screen's
      **Time range** property is the span it opens with (1 h, 6 h, 24 h
      or 7 days; it replaces a chart's own «visible span»). After a
      restart a screen opens at it again, as a local topic starts at its
      initial value.
    - **Two new gestures**, **Pinch in** (fingers drawn together) and
      **Pinch out** (drawn apart), offered on a screen's gestures like its
      swipes, only on a device whose touch reads two fingers: the 4.3B and
      the PaperS3 (GT911, up to five points; `schaltli-firmware`,
      `src/boards/waveshare4v3b/Gt911.h`, of which the firmware reads one
      today) and Android. The knob's CST816 reads one finger: no pinch
      there. As on a phone, the designer's default maps pinch in to widen
      and pinch out to narrow; a mapping can be changed like any other.
    - **Four new actions** on the screen's time range, offered wherever an
      action is chosen - a gesture, a hardware button, the knob's ring, a
      Button:
      - **Narrow the time range**: one step shorter, 7 days → 24 h → 6 h
        → 1 h.
      - **Widen the time range**: one step longer.
      - **Move the time range**, with a direction, earlier or later: by
        one range - at 24 h one day.
      - **Set the time range**, with a span: straight to 1 h, 6 h, 24 h
        or 7 days - for a Button Group «1 h | 24 h | 7 Tage».
    - **Steps, not free zooming**: a gesture fires an action once, so a
      pinch is one step. The device decides whether the step is animated.
      The proof of concept on the 4.3B therefore has to show that a pinch
      is recognised reliably, not that it can be followed smoothly.
      **Done on 2026-10-17** (`schaltli-firmware`,
      `src/project/PinchDetector.h`): a contact with two fingers on is a
      pinch and nothing else; decided at the lift from the fingers'
      distance, at least 60 px and a ratio of 0.75 or 1.33. By the user's
      hand, 20 pinches in and 20 out were all recognised, none mistaken;
      the second finger came 0 to 73 ms after the first, with 17 to 41
      two-finger samples a pinch and a travel of 300 px and more, far
      beyond the thresholds. Proved permanently by
      `hil/waveshare4v3b/pinch.js` and `test/test_pinch`.
    - **Dragging stays the chart's**: a scrollable chart still follows a
      finger, coasts and settles on whole days (decision 9), changing the
      same screen range. The actions are for whatever goes in steps.
    - Examples: the 4.3B's history page with pinch in/out on widen and
      narrow; the knob with its ring on «Move the time range» earlier and
      later and its press on «Set the time range» 24 h, back to today; any
      device with a Button Group setting the range.
    - **Zoomed out, a chart stays a curve** (user, 2026-10-17), however
      many days it shows; it does not turn into bars. Bars per day remain
      a chart of their own, in `per day` mode.
16. **All four devices in the first version** (user, 2026-10-17): 4.3B,
    knob, PaperS3 and Android together, before anyone sees it.

## Data model

Drafted on 2026-10-17 from the project's conventions (`components/project-editor.tsx`
`ScreenObject`, `ProjectScreen`, `HardwareButtonAction`; `lib/energy-diagram.ts`;
`lib/settable-level.ts`; `lib/themes.ts`; `lib/live-value.ts`;
`lib/device-description.ts`). Five implementations draw a chart - the
designer's preview, the 4.3B, the knob and the PaperS3 in
`schaltli-firmware`, and the Android app - and the recorder reads what it
needs; this is the contract between them.

**One rule first: a series is plain.** It holds its topic, kind, colour,
scale and annotations itself (decision 8, user 2026-10-17); what the
designer infers or takes over from a control it writes in when the series
is made, not when it is exported. The project's chart is what a device
gets, and five renderers read the same plain fields, none of them
following a reference or inferring anything.

### The chart object

An object of type `chart` (`lib/object-types.ts`), its settings in
`properties` as every object's are:

```ts
interface ChartProperties {
  mode: "time" | "per-day"
  days?: number                 // per-day: days shown, default 7
  scroll: boolean               // true on the charts of a proposed history page (decision 12)
  left: ChartAxis
  right?: ChartAxis             // used once a series is put on it
  zones?: ChartZone[]
  legend: "top-right" | "top-left" | "bottom" | "none"
  fontId?: string               // as the energy diagram's
  series: ChartSeries[]         // order is drawing and stacking order
}

interface ChartAxis {
  scale: "auto" | "fixed" | "at-least"  // auto starts at 0 when every value is >= 0
  min?: number                  // fixed, at-least
  max?: number
  unit?: string                 // "%", "°C", "W"; shown on the axis labels
}

interface ChartZone {
  axis: "left" | "right"
  from?: number                 // open below when absent
  to?: number                   // open above when absent
  color: string                 // a role or a hex, as every <part>Color
}
```

### A series

```ts
interface ChartSeries {
  id: string
  source:
    | { kind: "topic"; topic: string }                  // "topic" or "topic#path"
    | { kind: "balance" }       // minus the sum of the other series in its stack
  name: string
  valueKind: "level" | "power" | "state"                // inferred when made (decision 5)
  onMeans?: { op: ComparisonOperator; value: string }[] // state; any of them true means on
  invert?: boolean              // power: the topic counts the other way round («Reverse sign»)
  factor?: number               // shown = raw × factor, e.g. mV → V: 0.001; axis and legend alike
  unit?: string
  decimals?: number
  draw: "line" | "area" | "steps" | "band" | "stacked" | "bars"
  stack?: string                // series with the same stack id stack; a positive value
                                // above zero, a negative one below
  axis?: "left" | "right"       // default left
  color: string                 // a role or a hex
  range?: boolean               // the step's min and max as a faint band
  dailyValue?: "energy" | "decline" | "rise" | "mean" | "min" | "max" | "runtime"  // per-day
  annotations?: ("current" | "min" | "max" | "sum" | "runtime" | "starts")[]
}
```

- **What a control gives**, once, when the designer makes the series. A
  Bar, Gauge, Slider or Dial: its topic, the
  axis's fixed min and max from its outermost calibration points
  (`settableRange`), its fill colour; a Slider or Dial drawn as `steps`
  and named «Soll». A Switch: its topic, `state`, `band`, its on-value as
  `onMeans`. A value in a Text: its source and its number format's
  decimals. An energy diagram: one `stacked` `power` series per node, with
  the node's topic, name, colour role and «Reverse sign»; a sink's series
  inverted, so it lies below zero; the storage signed, above zero while
  it gives and below while it charges; the «rest» node a `balance`. A
  node added to the diagram later is not in the chart.
- **The kind decides the defaults**: `level` → `line`, annotations
  `current`; `power` → `area`, `sum`; `state` → `band`, `runtime`.
- **Invalid combinations are not stored**: `sum` only on `power`,
  `runtime` and `starts` only on `state`, `bars` and `dailyValue` only in
  `per-day` mode. The property panel offers only what fits.
- `factor` replaces calibration for a chart: a chart scales a value, it
  does not map it onto a bar's length. A Bar's calibration still gives the
  axis's range.

### The screen

```ts
interface ProjectScreen {
  // … as now …
  timeRange?: "1h" | "6h" | "24h" | "7d"  // the range it opens with (decision 15); default "24h"
}
```

A history page is an ordinary screen, placed below its page in the screen
graph (`docs/2026-10-17-screen-graph.md`), which also gives the swipes
between them and the pull tabs.

### Gestures and actions

- Two gesture ids beside `swipe-left` … `swipe-down` in `buttonActions`:
  **`pinch-in`** and **`pinch-out`**, offered only on a device whose DDF
  declares the feature `pinch` (decision 15).
- Four action types added to `HardwareButtonAction.type`:

  ```ts
  | "narrow-range"              // one step shorter: 7d → 24h → 6h → 1h
  | "widen-range"               // one step longer
  | "move-range"                // with rangeDirection: "earlier" | "later", by one range
  | "set-range"                 // with range: "1h" | "6h" | "24h" | "7d"
  ```

  with the fields `rangeDirection?` and `range?`. A new field rather than
  the existing `direction?: "up" | "down"` of `adjust-level`, which means
  something else. A Button takes the same actions.
- Defaults the designer sets when it makes a history page, on a device
  with `pinch`: `pinch-in` → `widen-range`, `pinch-out` → `narrow-range`. On the knob:
  the ring → `move-range` earlier/later, its press → `set-range` 24h.

### What a device declares

In its DDF, as with `local-topics` (`docs/2026-10-13-local-topics.md`):

- `supportedObjectTypes` contains `chart`;
- `supportedFeatures` contains **`charts`** - the screen's time range and
  the four range actions - and **`pinch`** where its touch
  reads two fingers (4.3B, PaperS3, Android; not the knob).

Deploy gates on them by name, as on `local-topics`: a project with a chart
goes only to a device that declares `charts`, after bringing an older
board up to date.

### What the recorder gets

On deploy the designer lays retained, per display:

```
schaltli/history/wants/<instance>
{ "series": [
    { "topic": "schaltli/state/tank/1/level", "kind": "level" },
    { "topic": "ble/charger/watts#value", "kind": "power" },
    { "topic": "schaltli/state/heater/power", "kind": "state",
      "onMeans": [{ "op": "==", "value": "on" }] } ] }
```

Topics only, raw: the recorder knows nothing of names, factors, colours
or annotations. A series of a `stacked` energy diagram is one entry per
node. An emptied want (a display deployed without charts) is cleared.

### What the recorder lays on the broker

Decision 7's topics, their payloads:

```
schaltli/history/<topic>/day/2026-10-14     level, power
{ "date": "2026-10-14", "step": 900, "mean": [...96], "min": [...96], "max": [...96] }

schaltli/history/<topic>/day/2026-10-14     state
{ "date": "2026-10-14", "on": [[420, 510], [1320, 1440]] }   // minutes since midnight

schaltli/history/<topic>/recent             the last 2 h at 1 min, same fields with "end" (unix s, the end of the
                                            last step) for "date"; a state's "on" in unix seconds
schaltli/history/<topic>/recent6            the last 48 h at 5 min
schaltli/history/<topic>/daily              { "days": [{ "date", "energyIn", "energyOut", "decline", "rise",
                                                         "mean", "min", "max", "runtime", "starts" }, ...30] }
schaltli/history/now                        { "time": <unix s>, "tz": "Europe/Zurich" }
```

`null` for a step nothing arrived in. A topic with `#path` is laid under
the full name, path included - with `~` for the `#`, which a published
topic name cannot hold: `ble/orion/in_volts#value` lies under
`schaltli/history/ble/orion/in_volts~value/…`. Numbers are raw, as they came.

## Left out

- Home Assistant's history API: a van with Home Assistant already has its
  history there; reading it needs a token and is for later.
- Exporting or downloading the history.
- More than 30 days.
- Forecasts of any kind (decision 14).
- Opening a history by a long press (decision 12).

## Still open from the ideas of 2026-10-17

- The starter project (#56) with its history pages ready made, so a
  Pekaway user swipes up without ever opening the designer.
- History as a source of values: the recorder publishing figures such as
  today's run time or solar yield as topics, for a Text or Live Icon on
  any page.
- Yesterday's curve as a faint shadow under today's.

## Open

- Whether Pekaway's Mosquitto keeps retained messages across its own
  restart (`persistence`); the recorder publishes the days it keeps again
  when it starts, so it matters only for the minutes in between.
- The recorder's memory on a 2 GB Pi (decision 6), to be measured.

recorder (`objekte/anzeigen.md`, `betrieb/`).
