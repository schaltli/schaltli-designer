# Guard

Spec drafted 2026-11-15 from a conversation with the user and the
prototype https://claude.ai/artifact/9a592QX6mXoSH4MLmphRz3 (variant 3,
«Schloss mit Abdeckung», chosen by the user on 2026-11-15). **Status:
approved by the user on 2026-11-15; not to be built yet.** Built after the starter and the film
(memory: starter-redesign-lauf, farbe-nach-wert-geparkt).

## Objective

Some controls in a van must not change by accident: the heater must not
come on from a brushed screen, the fridge or the router must not go off.
Today one tap switches anything. A **Guard** makes a control need a
deliberate gesture first: a cover is pushed up off the control, then the
control is tapped.

It is for whoever builds a screen with a heater, an inverter, a pump in
it, and for the passenger who doesn't know the screen.

## Decisions

All by the user, 2026-11-15, unless marked otherwise.

1. **Which types.** A Guard exists on the switch family and its
   relative: `switch`, `button-group`, `toggle-button` and `button`.
   Not on `slider`, `dial`, `text-field` or `time-field`.
2. **Per control, every state.** A Guard is on or off for the whole
   control. It protects every change the control can make - on, off,
   every option of a group, every press of a button. There is no guard
   per state, per option or per direction.
3. **On the control, not on the topic.** The Guard is a property of the
   object. Two controls on the same topic are guarded independently.
4. **At rest: only a lock.** A guarded control looks as it does without
   a Guard, plus a lock icon on it. Its state stays fully visible; no
   cover, no stripes, no frame. Where the lock sits:
   - Switch: on the knob, moving with it.
   - Toggle Button, Button: in the top-right corner, inside.
   - Button Group: a small round badge on the group's top-right corner.
   The lock is always shown; there is no option to hide it.
5. **Touching shows the cover.** A finger down anywhere on a guarded
   control shows the cover at once. Nothing is switched. The cover lies
   over the **whole control** - for a group over all its options, not
   over one segment.
6. **The cover's look.** It fills the control's outline (its corner
   radius), yellow diagonal stripes in two close yellows (calm, not
   hazard black), with the lock icon and a double arrow pointing up side
   by side in the middle, dark ink.
7. **The arrows move only while the cover can still be pushed.** From
   the moment the cover appears until it locks in, the double arrow
   bobs gently upwards (about 5 px, 1 s cycle). Once it is locked in,
   the arrows stand still.
8. **Push it up in one movement.** The cover slides straight up with
   the finger (2D: it moves, it doesn't tilt; never down) - as in the
   prototype with «Cover motion: Slide». Same on every device and in the
   preview. The gesture is one movement: finger down, push up.
   There is no tap-then-swipe.
9. **It locks in at half its height, while the finger is still
   down.** When the cover has travelled more than 45 % of the control's
   height, it locks in at once and snaps into its waiting position with
   a small overshoot (about 0.2 s). The control is now unlocked. The
   rest of that finger movement and the lift do nothing.
10. **Let go too early: it vanishes.** Lifting the finger before the
    cover has locked in - a plain tap or a push not far enough - makes
    the cover disappear at once (the same 0.2 s fall-and-fade as in 12),
    leaving the locked control as at rest. A short hint «Slide up to
    unlock» shows on a plain tap (prototype: «Deckel nach oben
    schieben»; the device's text is English like all UI).
11. **Waiting position.** The locked-in cover sits just above the
    control, half transparent (about 55 %), so the control and its
    label stay readable. It stays there for the relock time (13). While
    it waits, the control works as if it had no Guard: a tap switches,
    for a group a tap on any option, as often as wanted.
12. **Back down: fast, and gone.** When the relock time is over, the
    cover falls back onto the control in 0.2 s and fades out as it
    falls; only the lock remains.
13. **Relock time: 5 s, topped up to 2 s per change.** The cover waits
    5 s after locking in. Every change of state while it waits - by a
    tap - tops the time left up to at least 2 s: with
    4 s left it stays 4 s, with 0.5 s left it becomes 2 s (the user,
    2026-11-15). So a group can be stepped through without unlocking
    again, and the cover falls 2 s after the last change at the latest
    once the first 5 s are used up. Fixed on
    every device, not a project or object setting.
14. **The cover's yellow is fixed**, not a theme role: a guard looks
    the same on every screen and every theme.
15. **The Knob's encoder ignores the Guard.** The encoder operates no
    control: each detent runs the screen's button action (`button-0` /
    `button-1`, `pollEncoder` in schaltli-firmware/src/main.cpp). What it
    is set to do, it does, guard or not. Binding the encoder to a guarded
    switch is possible but pointless, and nobody would (the user,
    2026-11-15).
16. **Only touch on the control is guarded.** A state
    reported over MQTT changes the control as always, cover or not. Asked-value behaviour (the ring for
    "asked, not confirmed") is unchanged; it is drawn on the control,
    under a cover that is showing.

## Discarded on the way

So the next reader doesn't propose them again:

- A cover always lying over the control (glass with a striped rim, then
  flat): hides the state or clutters the screen at rest.
- Hold to switch with a filling ring: works, but nothing at rest shows
  which control is guarded, and it felt less deliberate.
- A countdown bar under the control, a cover or a lock sinking slowly
  back: busier than a cover that simply waits and drops.
- A cover that flips up on a top hinge (3D): costly or jerky on the ESP
  boards, so the preview would no longer be the device (the user chose
  slide, 2026-11-15).
- A guard per state or per transition, "leaving" a state, a guard per
  topic: more than a van needs (decision 2).

## Designer

- **Property panel**: one checkbox «Guard» for the four types, in the
  section the type's behaviour lives in. No further settings.
- **Canvas**: draws the lock at rest (decision 4), never the cover.
- **Preview** (handbuch/designer/vorschau.md): the full behaviour -
  cover, push, lock in, wait, drop - with mouse and touch, so a project
  can be tried before it is sent.
- **Convert to** between the switch types keeps `guard`.

## Export

`guard: true` on the object in project.json; absent means no Guard. No
looks are exported for the cover - it is the same on every control, so
each device draws it itself from the control's outline and the fixed
yellow (14).

Devices that don't know `guard` would ignore it and switch on a tap -
which is exactly the danger. So a device that draws the Guard declares
the feature `"guard"` in its DDF's `supportedFeatures` (device contract
§1), and Deploy treats a project with a Guard like any other feature the
device lacks (`unsupportedFeatures` in lib/deploy-plan.ts): it asks for
the update where the release has it, and refuses the deploy otherwise.
A project with a Guard never reaches a device that would ignore it.

## Devices

- **Firmware** (4", 4.3B, Knob, PaperS3): the lock is drawn as part of
  the control; the cover is one extra object per guarded control, shown
  on touch-down, moved with the finger, animated to the waiting position
  and back. Only 2D movement (translate and opacity) - cheap on LVGL.
  PaperS3: see edge case E7.
- **Android**: the same on the control's view.
- Every device keeps the same thresholds and times (45 %, 0.2 s, 5 s), so
  the gesture feels the same everywhere (memory: geraete-kein-zoo).

## Edge cases

Proposed by Claude on 2026-11-15 so the spec can be built without
further questions; they stand unless the user strikes or changes one.

- **E1 Small controls.** The cover locks in after 45 % of the control's
  height, but never after less than 20 px of travel (device pixels at
  the 4" screen's density, scaled like everything else). A flat strip
  still needs a real push. The waiting position stays "just above the
  control", whatever its height.
- **E2 Leaving the screen.** A screen change, the display going dark and
  a project reload lock every guarded control at once, without
  animation. Coming back finds them locked.
- **E3 Several at once.** Each guarded control has its own cover and its
  own time; two can be unlocked at the same time.
- **E4 Hidden while open.** A guarded control that is hidden while
  unlocked - its panel closes, a switcher shows another panel - locks at
  once. Shown again, it is locked.
- **E5 The hint.** «Slide up to unlock» appears after a plain tap only
  (finger moved less than 8 px), centred at the bottom of the screen,
  for 1.5 s; a new tap restarts it, never two at once. A device that has
  a toast of its own uses it.
- **E6 Swipes.** A touch that starts on a guarded control belongs to
  the cover: it never pages the screen, whichever way it moves. A
  sideways movement simply doesn't push the cover. A touch that starts
  elsewhere pages as before, also across a guarded control. A second
  finger is ignored while the first is on a cover.
- **E7 PaperS3.** The same gesture with as few refreshes as possible:
  finger down draws the cover (one partial refresh); the cover does not
  follow the finger; once the finger has travelled far enough the cover
  is drawn in its waiting position (one refresh); lifting early removes
  it (one refresh). No bobbing arrows, no fade: the cover drops as one
  refresh. Times and thresholds as everywhere.
- **E8 Pressed look.** While the cover is showing, the control's own
  pressed look (a button's or toggle button's state layer) is not
  drawn - the cover is what is being touched.
- **E9 The lock's size.** The lock scales with the control like its
  icon does: on a switch about 40 % of the knob, in a corner about the
  control's small text size, the group's badge about 20 px at the 4"
  density. Never so small it can't be seen at arm's length.
- **E10 Designer preview.** The preview uses the same thresholds and
  times as the devices; the mouse acts as one finger.

## Tests

- `e2e/`: the property, export of `guard`, the canvas lock, Convert to
  keeping it, and the preview gesture (touch down shows cover, early
  lift removes it, push past 45 % unlocks, tap switches, cover drops
  after 5 s, a change with less than 2 s left moves the drop to 2 s
  after it, a change with more left doesn't move it), the 20 px minimum
  (E1), locking on screen change and on hiding (E2, E4), the hint after
  a plain tap only (E5). Deploy refuses a Guard project for a device
  without the `guard` feature.
- HIL: a guarded specimen in `hil/conformance/specimens.js`; per device,
  a tap does not switch, a push then a tap does, a swipe starting on the
  guarded control does not page (E6), a screen change locks it (E2).
- `npm run test:quick` (touches the project format and every device).
