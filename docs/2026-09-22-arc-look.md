# The ring, brought alongside the bar

2026-09-22. The gauge and the dial were the last controls still wearing the
look everything else left behind on 2026-09-19: a flat-ended band, a track in
a colour of its own, a white wedge for the setpoint, a background nobody
asked for. This is what they look like now, and why.

The decisions below were taken one at a time, in an interview, before any of
it was written. What is recorded here is the answer and the reason - not the
options, which are gone.

## What the eye sees

**Rounded ends, the bar's way.** The track is a pill: a half-disc at each end
of the scale, and a straight radial edge where the fill meets the rest. Two
round ends facing each other read as a gap, which is why the inner edge stays
straight - the same reasoning as the bar's segments
(docs/2026-09-19-slider-look.md).

The band is defined as *every point within half a thickness of its centreline
arc*. That is the literal definition of a pill, and it is worth stating
because the first attempt did something else: it cut the band with a
half-plane through each end so the rounding would stay inside the declared
angles. On a 270 degree dial that plane also slices through the far side of
the ring, and 45 degrees of band disappeared at each end. The picture showed
it immediately; no test would have.

The cost of the honest definition is that the ends reach half a thickness
past `minAngle` and `maxAngle`, exactly as a bar's pill reaches past its own
run. A scale whose gap is narrower than one thickness therefore closes up,
and a ring that goes all the way round has no ends to round at all.

**The setpoint is the slider's handle.** A pill lying across the band, in the
fill's own colour, standing out of it on both sides, with a gap cut either
side of it - and the gap takes its pixels from the ring, not from the handle,
which is what separates the two. Its measurements are the slider's, unchanged:
eleven quarters of the thickness long, an eleventh of that wide, three
twenty-seconds of gap. Two clamps a straight bar never needs: never longer
than twice the centreline's radius (or it reaches through the middle of the
dial and out the other side), and never longer than a third of the scale's
own run.

The handle is tested *before* the ring's own radii, and the pixel short cut
at the top of the rasterizer allows for its reach. The first version did
neither, and the handle came out as a sliver trapped inside the band.

**The ring moves in to make room for the handle.** With the band touching
the object's edge - which is where a ring has always sat - the outer half of
the handle falls outside the object, and an object that draws past its own
rectangle is clipped by the designer's buffer, by the app's box and by the
firmware's object rect alike. Found on the live canvas, after the pictures
had already been signed off, which is a good argument for looking at the
thing in the tool rather than only in a render.

So the room is taken out of the radius: half the handle's overhang, reserved
whenever the object CAN have a handle - a write topic or a setpoint topic -
rather than while one is being drawn, so the ring does not jump inwards the
moment a value arrives. The bar reserves its handle's room the same way: its
slot is as tall as the handle, whatever the track's thickness. On an object
too small to give up the room, the reservation gives way before the band
does.

**One colour, everything else derived.** The unfilled track is the fill's
colour mixed halfway into what the ring stands on - `levelTrackLook`, the
same function the bar's track uses, so a bar and a ring on one screen are one
family. Where that mixture cannot be told from the background - all of 1 bit -
the track becomes an **outline**: the band's own outer pixel along each radius
and around each cap, open where the band was cut. A track you cannot see is
not a track; a value you cannot see is a lie, so the fill and the handle stay
solid.

**The handle's colour says whether it can be moved.** Decided on the picture,
after the rest: a handle a finger can move keeps the bar's own colour -
handle and filled track are one object that the gap separates (2026-09-19) -
and one that only reports the installation's target takes the track's colour
and steps back. The user, seeing it: "seine dimmed farbe sagt mir dass ich
ihn nicht bewegen kann".

This refines decision 4 of the slider look rather than overturning it: the
SHAPE of a handle is the same wherever it appears, which is what that
decision was about. Only the colour now carries one more thing.

On a 1-bit panel all handles stay solid: the track is an outline there, so
there is no quiet colour to step back into. It costs nothing, because that
device declares `bar` and `gauge` and neither `slider` nor `dial` - nothing
on that glass can be moved by a finger, so every handle it draws is a
reported target already. A 1-bit device with touch would want an outlined
handle, in the language its own track already speaks.

**The numbers say what the bar says.** The big one is the commanded value -
where the handle points - and the measured one appears under it in brackets,
smaller, only when it differs. A ring puts them one above the other rather
than side by side: it has the room, and it has no header line to lay them out
on.

Not adopted: the bar's header line with a name and an icon. A ring has a
better place for its number, which is the middle.

## What the author sets

| | before | now |
|---|---|---|
| thickness | `barThickness` (bar) / `thickness` (ring) | **`thickness`** on both; `barThickness` still read |
| direction | `barDirection` / `direction` | **`direction`** on both; `barDirection` still read |
| track colour | `trackColor` | gone - derived |
| setpoint | `markerColor`, `markerWidth` | gone - the handle is the fill's colour, its size follows the thickness |
| background | `backgroundColor` | gone - the ring floats on the screen |

The default is 16 on both. It was going to stay at the ring's own 22 - a
ring was thought to need more presence - and that survived exactly as long
as it took to put a dial and a slider on one screen: at 22 against 16 they
are not the same control in two shapes, they are two controls. A dial with
no thickness of its own is drawn at 16 from now on.

And the handle is the same width on both, which it was not: a bar derived
its handle's width from the slot, which a short object clamps, so a 40-unit
bar drew a 3-wide handle beside a ring's 4. Width and gap now follow the
length the THICKNESS asks for on both shapes; only the length itself gives
way to an object with no room. What is short of room is the space along the
handle, never across it. Measured after the change: 4 px on each.

Old projects are **not** touched. The retired values stay in the file and are
no longer read, exactly as the bar's own `backgroundColor` and marker styles
were left in place on 2026-09-19. Nothing rewrites a file someone else owns.

The bar's panel lost three dead controls in the same round - marker style,
marker width, marker colour - which had been offering choices that nothing
had read since the slider look.

## Where this has to be copied

The rasterizer exists **four** times: `lib/arc-raster.ts` here,
`ArcRaster.cpp` in `screenbee-firmware` (knob, 4.3B, PaperS3),
`ArcRaster.cpp` in `MqttEPaperDisplay2` (the only 1-bit device, and so the
only one that draws the outline at all), and `ArcRaster.kt` in the Android
app. All four are to follow; the designer is the master and was signed off
first, on the pictures.

Until they do, `android-unit` fails: the golden file
(`hil/android/fixtures/build-arc-golden.js`) was re-recorded with the new
bands the moment the designer changed, deliberately, so that the divergence
is loud rather than quietly passing against a stale expectation. Three cases
were added for what is new - a scale with both ends rounded and nothing in
it, a handle standing out of the ring, and a framed track - and each is
recorded whole, because a handle is a few pixels wide and a row-and-diagonal
sample misses it completely. The first recording of them proved that by
reporting `handle=0`.
