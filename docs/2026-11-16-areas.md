# Areas: one kind of layout region

Spec drafted 2026-11-16 from a conversation with the user. **Status: approved
by the user on 2026-11-16, step 3 included. Decisions 1-11 were proposed and
approved with the spec; 12-16 are the user's answers of the same day.**

## Objective

The designer has too many things that hold other objects and place them, and
each does it a little differently (the user, 2026-11-16: «zu kompliziert, zu
viele Klassen, die die Funktion eines Panels haben»). Whoever builds a screen
has to know which of them can dock, which can pad, which can carry a look, and
wraps one in another to get what the first cannot do.

After this, there are two ideas:

- An **area** holds objects and places them, free or docked.
- A **table** arranges areas in a grid.

The screen, a switcher's panel, a table's cell and today's Free area are all
the same area, with the same properties in the same sections.

It is for whoever builds screens in the designer. A device sees none of it:
areas and tables are dissolved on export, as today.

## Today

Five things hold children:

| Region | Places its children | Its own size | Dock, Position, Padding | Look |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Screen | free, x/y | the device's | no | no |
| Panel (in a switcher) | free, x/y | the switcher's | no | no |
| Free, with look «Card» | free, x/y | its own, at least its content | yes | yes |
| Table cell | one object, «Cell» Horizontal/Vertical | its column's and row's | its own «Cell» section | no |
| Group | not at all | its children's | no | no |

What this costs, on the user's screen «MaxxFan» of «VanPi-Starter»
(2026-11-16):

- **A box per cell.** Three of the top table's five cells hold a Free card
  stretched both ways, because a cell cannot carry a look or hold two objects.
- **A box per panel.** Each of the switcher's two panels holds exactly one
  Free card with everything in it, because a panel cannot dock.
- **Two section names for one thing.** «Cell» in a table and «Position» in a
  Free area both offer Horizontal and Vertical with Left, Center, Right,
  Stretch. They are two properties (`cell.anchor`, `anchor`) and two pieces of
  code.
- **Three kept sizes.** An object stretched in a cell keeps
  `cell.drawnWidth/drawnHeight`; a docked one keeps `ownSize`; a table keeps
  column sizes set by hand and `widthMm`. The card «Deckel» kept 266 px
  nobody could see or change, and the table stood 62 px over the screen's
  edge. «Min W» and «Min H» (built 2026-11-16) make the first of the three
  visible; they do not make it one.

## Decisions (proposed)

1. **One area.** An area has: its children; `padding`; `gap`; an optional
   look (none, «Card»); a fill and a stroke of its own. It clips what it holds
   when it can be seen, as a card does today (docs/2026-11-03-card-clip.md).
   Its children stand free at x/y with a Position (Horizontal, Vertical), or
   are docked (Top, Bottom, Left, Right, Fill). This is today's Free area,
   word for word; nothing about it changes.
2. **Four places it appears.** The screen; a switcher's panel; a table's
   cell; standing alone, placed like any object (today's «Free» tool, renamed «Area»,
   and «Card»; decision 15).
3. **Where its size comes from** is the only difference between the four:
   - Screen: the device's panel, at least.
   - Panel: the switcher's, at least.
   - Cell: its column's width and its row's height.
   - Standing alone: its own, typed or dragged.
   In all four the area is at least as large as its content needs
   (lib/free-min-size.ts), and grows to that - the screen's area too (the
   user, 2026-11-16): the designer already lets one draw past the screen's
   edge, and one sees what one does. A screen's area that has grown past the
   panel is drawn past it in the editor; the device shows what lies on its
   panel, as today. So no area ever cuts or leaves out what is docked in it,
   and the rule «no smaller than its content» has no exception. See E1.
4. **A table arranges areas.** A cell is an area. Columns are as wide as
   their widest cell needs, rows as high as their highest; spans, sizes set
   by hand, `Gap`, `Width` and `Equal columns` stay as they are.
5. **A cell with one object is today's cell.** The object is docked Fill in
   its cell: it takes the room if it can stretch that way, otherwise it stands
   in it by its Horizontal and Vertical. That is how Dock already treats an
   object across its dock, and it is exactly what «Cell» does today. Nobody
   building a table of texts and switches sees a difference.
6. **A cell can hold more.** A second object dropped into an occupied cell
   stands free in it where it was let go, as in a Free area today, and can be
   docked. No Free area is needed in between.
7. **A cell can carry the look.** A card in a table is a cell with look
   «Card», not an object stretched into the cell.
8. **One section, «Position».** Every object directly in an area shows
   «Position» with Dock, Horizontal and Vertical - on a screen, in a panel,
   in a cell, in a standing area. The section «Cell» goes; how many cells an
   object's cell spans stays on the handles and the label.
9. **One kept size.** An object has a width and a height. Where its area or
   its table gives it more room than that (Stretch in a cell, across a dock),
   Frame shows them as «Min W» and «Min H»: the least it asks for. One
   property for it, `properties.ownSize`, replacing `cell.drawnWidth`,
   `cell.drawnHeight` and today's docked `ownSize`. Absent means: what its
   content needs.
10. **Group stays.** It holds objects together and places nothing.
11. **No device change.** The export dissolves areas and tables and hands a
    device placed objects, a `box` for an area that can be seen and `clip` on
    what it holds, as today. No new feature in any DDF, no firmware or app
    release.

## Order of work

Each step ships alone and leaves every project looking as it did.

1. **Panel and screen are areas.** Dock, Position and Padding on objects
   directly on a screen and in a panel. On load, a panel that holds exactly
   one Free area filling it takes that area's properties and children; the
   Free area is gone.
2. **One kept size.** `ownSize` everywhere; «Min W»/«Min H» wherever a
   stretch gives more room, docked objects included.
3. **The cell is an area.** «Cell» becomes «Position»; a look and padding on
   a cell; several objects in a cell. On load, a Free area stretched both ways
   in its cell becomes the cell.

Step 3 is the largest and holds the open questions. Steps 1 and 2 are worth
having without it.

## Data model

```ts
// An area's own properties - today's Free area's, unchanged:
properties.look?: "card"
properties.padding?: number      // mm
properties.gapMm?: number
properties.fillColor / strokeColor / strokeWidth / cornerRadius

// On an object directly in any area - today only in a Free area:
properties.anchor?: { x?: AnchorX; y?: AnchorY }
properties.dock?: Dock
properties.ownSize?: { width?: number; height?: number }

// A screen and a panel carry the area's properties on themselves.

// Step 3 - a table's children are its cells:
{ type: "cell", properties: { row, column, columnSpan?, rowSpan?, ...area }, children: [...] }
```

`properties.cell` on an object (row, column, spans, anchor, drawnWidth,
drawnHeight) goes with step 3: the place moves to the cell, the anchor to
`properties.anchor`, the drawn size to `ownSize`.

## Old projects

On load, in `migrateProject` (lib/object-types.ts), step by step as above.
The rule for all of it: **nothing moves.** A project's device zip, exported
before and after the migration, is byte for byte the same.

- An object in a cell: its cell becomes a `cell` area holding it, docked
  Fill, with `cell.anchor` as its `anchor`.
- `cell.drawnWidth/drawnHeight` larger than what the content needs become
  `ownSize`; equal or smaller, they are dropped.
- A Free area stretched both ways in its cell becomes the cell: look,
  padding, gap, colours and children move up. Its `ownSize` becomes the
  cell's, so a card pulled taller on purpose stays that tall - and now shows
  it as «Min H».
- A Free area not stretched in its cell stays a standing area in the cell.
- A panel holding exactly one Free area that fills it takes it over (step 1).
- A project saved by the new designer does not open in an older one. The
  format version goes up, as with earlier layout changes.

## Edge cases

- **E1. A screen's content needs more than the panel.** The screen's area
  grows to the right and downwards, its top left corner staying. What is
  docked Right or Bottom then lies on the grown edge, partly or wholly past
  the panel, and the editor shows it there. The device draws what lies on
  its panel and nothing else, as it does today with an object placed past
  the edge. `dockedOut` and cutting along the dock are not needed for a
  screen - nor, with this, for any area, since every area now grows; they
  stay only for projects that still carry them, and go on load once the
  area has grown.
- **E1a. Objects standing free on a screen may lie anywhere,** left of it
  and above it too, wholly or partly past its edge, as today (the user,
  2026-11-16). This is the one thing a screen does not share with the other
  areas: a standing area, a cell and a panel move a free object in from
  their padding and grow to hold it (lib/free-min-size.ts); a screen does
  neither. Only what is docked on a screen can make its area grow, and it
  grows to the right and downwards only. Position (Left, Right, Center,
  Stretch) on a screen measures from the panel's edges, not from the grown
  area's.
- **E2. A panel holds what is in it.** Today a panel has the switcher's
  size whatever stands in it: selected, it shows its border, and some of its
  controls float outside (the user, 2026-11-16). As an area it is at least
  as large as its content needs, and the switcher is as large as its largest
  panel - standing free too, not only in a table or docked as today. An
  object let go past a panel's right or bottom edge makes the panel, and
  with it the switcher, grow; past its left or top edge it is moved in, as
  in a standing area. Dragged smaller, the switcher stops where every panel
  still holds its content. Parking an object outside is for the screen only
  (E1a).
- **E2a. A project whose panels have controls floating outside.** On load
  the switcher grows to hold them: to the right and downwards by growing,
  to the left and upwards by moving its corner and its children's places
  with it, so every control stays where it was on the glass. Such a
  project's export is not the same bytes as before - the switcher is larger
  - and the test «nothing moves» compares these by where each object lies,
  not by bytes. No device clips a panel's objects to the switcher (read
  in the code, 2026-11-16: firmware `renderTabControl`, Android
  `TabControlView`), so they are drawn whole today. But on the 4" and 4.3B
  a switcher changing its panel redraws only its own rectangle
  (src/boards/rgbpanel/main.cpp, «the whole switcher anew»): what the old
  panel had outside stays on the glass and what the new one has outside is
  not drawn until something else redraws there. A switcher that holds its
  panels ends that without a firmware change.
- **E3. An empty cell** is an area with nothing in it; it stays, as today,
  until its row or column is empty.
- **E4. The last object taken out of a cell that has a look.** The cell
  stays, an empty card. Without a look, today's rule: an empty row or column
  goes, a table with one object left dissolves.
- **E5. A table with one cell left that has a look or several objects** does
  not dissolve into its object; it becomes a standing area with the cell's
  properties and children.
- **E6. Dropping onto an occupied cell.** Without Alt the object goes into
  the cell under the pointer and stands free there (decision 6). With Alt it
  snaps beside, as today. «Paste Replace» keeps replacing.
- **E7. Master screens.** A master's objects are placed on the master's own
  area; a screen showing the master lays out its own objects on its own. The
  two do not dock against each other.
- **E8. The round knob.** Its area is the square around the circle, as its
  screen is today.
- **E9. A text in a cell** spans its cell and aligns its words by its own
  text alignment, as today (docs/2026-11-03-anchor-dock.md).
- **E10. Prebuilts and templates** (lib/bausteine.ts, lib/block-card.ts)
  build cells with a look where they build stretched cards today.

## Not in scope

- Any change to what a device draws or receives.
- Padding per side, a gap per axis.
- Tables in tables as a tool of their own; a table still goes into a cell by
  standing in the cell's area.
- Leaving objects that lie wholly past the panel out of the export. Today
  every object is exported and the device clips while drawing. Leaving them
  out would save files, but an object parked past the edge can still be a
  hardware button's target, and the export would no longer be the same bytes
  as before. A question for another day.
- Removing the Box. It is a drawing, not an area, and stays.
- The Group.
- Flow or wrapping layouts.

## Decided (the user, 2026-11-16)

12. **A cell is a level of its own only where it has to be:** when it has a
    look or holds several objects. Then it is table, cell, object, with Esc
    going back up. A cell holding one object without a look is skipped: a
    click selects the table and a double click the object, as today, and
    that object's panel shows its cell's look and padding under «Position».
13. **Dock is always shown.** An object alone in its cell shows «Dock: Fill»
    under «Position», and it can be changed. The same three fields wherever
    an object stands.
14. **Docking keeps no size.** A docked object has a «Min W» or «Min H» only
    when one was typed. A card can be made as narrow as its content's words,
    as today.
15. **«Free» becomes «Area».** The tool, the object list and the panel's
    section say «Area»; «Card» stays the name of the tool that places an
    area with the look, and of the look. A cell is «Cell» in the object
    list, with the look «Card». The object's type in a project file stays
    `free`: a name in the interface, not a new format. The handbook follows;
    the starter film and forum posts say «Free» and are left as they are.
16. **Parking is for the screen.** Objects standing free may lie anywhere on
    a screen (E1a); a panel grows to hold what is in it (E2).

## Open questions

1. **Changing «Dock: Fill» on an object alone in its cell** (decision 13):
   None lets it stand free in the cell at its place, Top/Bottom/Left/Right
   put it at that edge. Both work as in any area. Whether a cell whose one
   object stands free should then count as «more than a slot» for decision
   12 is to be settled while planning.

## Handbook

«Free» becomes «Area» wherever the handbook quotes it
(e2e/handbook-labels.spec.ts will say where). `handbuch/objekte/anordnen.md`
is rewritten around the two ideas: the area
first, then the table as a grid of areas. Sections «Panel», «Free» and «Card»
shrink to where an area appears. `designer/screens.md`,
`designer/baugruppen.md` and `designer/index.md` follow where they name
«Cell» or explain a card in a table.

## Success criteria

- «MaxxFan» rebuilt needs no Free area: three cells with the look, two panels
  that dock their own content.
- The property panel has one section for placing an object, with the same
  three fields wherever the object stands.
- No size that decides a layout is kept where the panel does not show it.
- Every existing project exports the same bytes before and after.
