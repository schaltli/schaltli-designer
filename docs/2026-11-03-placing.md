# Placing

Spec for module `placing`, drafted 2026-11-03. It revises the snapping
decision of docs/2026-10-09-snap-tables.md (2026-10-10: snapping on, Ctrl to
place freely) and takes in `drop-into-free` (docs/2026-11-03-drop-into-free.md,
built on its branch, not merged). **Status: approved by the user on 2026-11-03.**

## Objective

Three ways of placing live side by side - free with Ctrl, snapping into
tables otherwise, into a free area by the middle of what is dragged - and the
user, trying them, was not sure one gets along with them without the
handbook (2026-11-03). Snapping on by default surprises: when it came, seven
tests that put objects side by side on purpose had to hold Ctrl.

After this module there is one rule, the same for a dragged object, a new
one placed with a tool, a carried row and a block:

- **No key:** it goes where the pointer is - into the innermost free area or
  card there, or into the empty table cell there; anywhere else it lies free.
- **Alt held:** it builds tables, as snapping does today - a new column or
  row, a table with the object beside it, a row into a table.
- **A hint at the pointer** names what happens and shows the Alt key drawn
  as a key, so nobody needs the handbook to find it.

Decisions (the user, 2026-11-03): the pointer decides, not the middle of the
object; table building with Alt (Shift if Chrome's handling of Alt gets in
the way); the same rule for rows and blocks; `drop-into-free` goes into this
module.

## Behaviour

### What decides

- The **pointer**, where the object is held - for a new object, where the
  tool's object hangs at the pointer (by its middle, as today).

### No key

- **Over a free area or a card** (open or not, at any depth, through
  tables and groups, not into a switcher's panels from outside): into the
  innermost one under the pointer, outlined while carried, standing where it
  was let go. It starts as a new object there: no Position, no Dock.
- **Over an empty cell** of a table put together by snapping: into that
  cell, the cell lit green as today. A card in that cell is a free area and
  takes it instead, when the pointer is over the card.
- **Anywhere else** - over the screen, over an occupied cell, beside a
  table or another object: free, where it was let go, in the open space (the
  screen, a panel, an open area). Nothing snaps.
- **Out:** an object of an open free area let go with the pointer outside
  it goes to whatever is under the pointer by the same rule - another area,
  an empty cell, or free in the space around.
- **Out of a table:** as today an object dragged out of a table is carried
  as an outline; let go, it goes by the same rule.

### Alt held

- Exactly what snapping does today (docs/2026-10-09-snap-tables.md): by the
  object's edges, a new column or row beside or between, a table of two
  with a free neighbour, the nearest edge of an occupied cell; the Row tool's
  row and a block's row into the table by role; a block of several parts as
  a card into a table. Lines and cells drawn as today.
- Inside a free area, Alt builds tables among the area's objects (the area
  under the pointer is the space, as with no key).
- Alt can be pressed or let go while carrying; what is shown follows.

### The hint

- While carrying - a drag, a tool's object, a row, a block - a chip at the
  pointer says what letting go does now, in a word or two (English, UI):
  «Into Card», «Into cell», «Free»; with Alt «New column», «New row»,
  «Table», «Into table».
- Where Alt would do something else than letting go now, the chip shows the
  key drawn as a keycap, «Alt», beside what it would do, faint; with Alt
  held, the keycap is pressed and the text strong.
- Nothing is shown where Alt would change nothing.

### Ctrl

- Ctrl no longer changes placing. Ctrl+click still adds to the selection.

### Unchanged

- Snapping to guides (edges and middles of other objects) while dragging
  free.
- Undo: one step per placing.
- Escape while carrying puts everything back.
- What a device receives: placing only changes where objects sit in the
  tree.

## Not in scope

- Reparenting into a group or a switcher's panel by dropping (panels are
  opened, as today).
- A setting to bring back snapping by default.

## Code

- `lib/drop-into-free.ts` (from `drop-into-free`): the target under a point
  - innermost free area, or an empty cell - instead of the middle and the
  inset zone.
- `components/canvas/canvas.tsx`: every place that asks `e.ctrlKey ||
  e.metaKey` to turn snapping off asks for Alt to turn it on instead
  (drag, tool placing, Row tool, block, out of a table); the area and the
  cell targets by the pointer; the chip with the keycap; Alt's default
  (Chrome focusing its menu on a lone Alt) prevented while carrying.
- `components/project-editor.tsx`: the `moveIntoAreaOf` callback from
  `drop-into-free`, also for an empty cell (via `applySnapDrop` with a cell
  target).
- Tests' helper `placingFreely` (holds Ctrl) replaced by `buildingTables`
  (holds Alt), used where a test builds a table by dragging.

## Testing

- `e2e/drop-into-free.spec.ts` and `e2e/drop-into-free-canvas.spec.ts`
  (from `drop-into-free`) moved to the pointer.
- `e2e/placing.spec.ts` (new): without a key, dropped beside an object it
  lies free (no table); over an empty cell into the cell; over an occupied
  cell free; with Alt a new column, a table of two, a row by role; Alt
  pressed and let go while carrying changes the line shown; a tool's new
  object and a block by the same rule; the chip's text and keycap in each
  case.
- The 12 specs using `placingFreely` and those that build tables by
  dragging (snap-table-canvas, snap-table-rows, place-by-dragging,
  card-grid-canvas, card-blocks, bausteine, ...) moved to the new rule.

## Handbook

`handbuch/objekte/anordnen.md` «Zusammenstecken», «Free» (in and out),
«Card» (a grid of cards with Alt), the Row tool; `handbuch/designer/
bausteine.md` (blocks placed and snapped). Through `maettel-humanizer`.

## Boundaries

- **Always:** one rule for every kind of carrying; `test:quick` per change;
  handbook in the same work.
- **Ask first:** a key other than Alt or Shift; keeping any default snapping.
- **Never:** a change to what a device receives.

## Success criteria

- [ ] Without a key: into the free area or empty cell under the pointer,
      else free; nothing snaps into a table otherwise
- [ ] With Alt: every table building of today, for drag, tool, row and block
- [ ] The chip says what happens and shows Alt as a key where it matters
- [ ] Alt does not take the browser's focus while carrying
- [ ] Handbook updated; `test:quick` green apart from known load failures
